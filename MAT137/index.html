02

Source Spine

MAT137 is not a textbook course in the usual sense. The course spine is Alfonso Gracia-Saz's inverted-classroom material: videos, unit guides, classroom questions, problem sets, and past assessments. This guide follows that order closely and expands each unit into a self-contained reading chapter.

How this guide follows the course materials
Primary source

Use the MAT137 video playlist and the Alfonso calculus question bank as the source of truth. The local pages do not replace them; they explain the same topics in a slower written format and add worked proof models.

Unit alignment

Units 1-6 match the differential-calculus half: proof language, limits, derivatives, transcendental functions, MVT, and applications. Units 7-14 match the integral and series half: Riemann sums, FTC, integration methods, applications, sequences, improper integrals, series, and Taylor series.

What to produce

For every unit, write a one-page definition sheet, a theorem sheet with proof sketches, a mistake log, and at least two fully written solutions from the official question bank before moving to old tests.

VIDEO

Watch before reading

Pause whenever a definition appears. Rewrite it in quantifier form and predict the first line of a proof using that definition.

GUIDE

Read the local chapter

Use the local page to fill gaps: theorem statements, proof templates, worked examples, and common traps.

CLASS

Do classroom questions

Use Alfonso's question bank immediately after each unit. Treat these as active recall, not examples to skim.

TEST

Finish with past tests

After every two or three units, attempt old MAT137 problems under time pressure and rewrite weak solutions cleanly.

Practice Plan

The unit pages already include local practice. Use this ladder to turn that practice into course-level mastery.

01

Definition drills

Before solving, write the exact definition needed: limit, continuity, derivative, integrability, convergence, or uniform convergence.

02

One proof, three versions

Redo important proofs from memory, then write a shorter exam version, then write a version explaining every quantifier choice.

03

Mixed-topic sets

Every weekend, mix one problem from each previous unit so limits, derivatives, integrals, and series do not live in separate boxes.

04

Reflection log

Record the exact line where each failed proof broke. MAT137 rewards fixing proof habits more than memorizing more formulas.

Course-wide challenge bank

Use these after the relevant units. They are meant to force proof choices across the whole MAT137 sequence.

Challenge 1 · Quantifiers

Negate the statement: "$f$ is continuous at $a$." Then explain in one sentence what the negation says geometrically.

Solution sketch
Continuity is $\forall\varepsilon>0\ \exists\delta>0\ \forall x\, (|x-a|<\delta\Rightarrow |f(x)-f(a)|<\varepsilon)$. Its negation is $\exists\varepsilon>0\ \forall\delta>0\ \exists x$ with $|x-a|<\delta$ but $|f(x)-f(a)|\ge\varepsilon$. No matter how close one zooms near $a$, some point stays visibly away from $f(a)$.
Challenge 2 · Epsilon-delta

Prove from the definition that $\lim_{x\to2}(3x+1)=7$.

Solution sketch
$|(3x+1)-7|=3|x-2|$. Given $\varepsilon>0$, choose $\delta=\varepsilon/3$. If $0<|x-2|<\delta$, then $|(3x+1)-7|<3\delta=\varepsilon$.
Challenge 3 · MVT application

Let $0<a<b$. Prove that $\dfrac{b-a}{b}<\ln b-\ln a<\dfrac{b-a}{a}$.

Solution sketch
Apply the Mean Value Theorem to $\ln x$ on $[a,b]$. There is $c\in(a,b)$ with $\ln b-\ln a=(b-a)/c$. Since $a<c<b$, we have $1/b<1/c<1/a$, and multiplying by $b-a>0$ gives the result.
Challenge 4 · Series decision

Determine whether $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{n}{n^3+1}$ converges.

Solution sketch
For large $n$, $\dfrac{n}{n^3+1}\sim\dfrac1{n^2}$. Use limit comparison with $\sum 1/n^2$: the ratio tends to $1$, so the series converges.
