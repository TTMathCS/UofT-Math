Sequences & Cauchy Sequences

Sequences in their rigorous form, plus the Cauchy criterion — convergence detected without knowing the limit. This is the deep idea the analysis stream adds beyond ordinary calculus.

Why it matters & where it comes from
Why it exists

Sometimes you must know a sequence converges without naming its limit. The Cauchy criterion does exactly that, and it is how the completeness of $\mathbb{R}$ is most usefully expressed.

In the real world

Every iterative algorithm is a sequence — Newton's method, fixed-point iteration, machine-learning training loops. "Cauchy" is the practical test that successive iterates stop changing.

Story & open questions

Cantor and Dedekind used Cauchy sequences to construct $\mathbb{R}$ in the 1870s. Zeno's 450 BC paradox of Achilles and the tortoise is resolved by the limit of such a sequence.

By the end you can
  • State the ε–N definition and prove sequence limits.
  • Apply the Monotone Convergence Theorem.
  • State the Cauchy criterion and why it matters.

Limits and monotone convergence

Definition

$\lim a_n=L$: $\forall\varepsilon>0\ \exists N$ with $n\ge N\Rightarrow |a_n-L|<\varepsilon$.

Monotone Convergence

An increasing sequence bounded above converges to its supremum.

Cauchy sequences

Definition Cauchy

$(a_n)$ is Cauchy if $\forall\varepsilon>0\ \exists N$ with $m,n\ge N\Rightarrow|a_m-a_n|<\varepsilon$.

Theorem

In $\mathbb{R}$, a sequence converges iff it is Cauchy. (This is the completeness of $\mathbb{R}$ restated.)

Common pitfalls

• "Cauchy" compares terms to each other, not to a limit.
• Cauchy implies convergent only because $\mathbb{R}$ is complete — it fails in $\mathbb{Q}$.
• A bounded sequence need not converge unless it is also monotone or has a convergent subsequence.

Practice

Practice 1

Prove $\lim\dfrac{n}{n+1}=1$.

Answer
$\left|\tfrac{n}{n+1}-1 \right|=\tfrac1{n+1}<\varepsilon$ for $n>1/\varepsilon$.
Practice 2

Show $a_1=\sqrt2,\ a_{n+1}=\sqrt{2+a_n}$ converges and find the limit.

Hint
Increasing and bounded by $2$; limit solves $L=\sqrt{2+L}$, so $L=2$.
Practice 3

Why is $a_n=(-1)^n$ not Cauchy?

Answer
Consecutive terms differ by $2$, so no tail stays within $\varepsilon=1$.
