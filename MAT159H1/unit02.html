Improper Integrals

Integrals over infinite intervals, or of functions that blow up, defined carefully as limits. The recurring question: does the limit exist?

Why it matters & where it comes from
Why it exists

Many vital integrals run to infinity or have a singularity — probability distributions, decaying signals. Improper integrals give them a rigorous meaning.

In the real world

The Gaussian integral $\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-x^2}\,dx=\sqrt{\pi}$ normalizes the bell curve; Laplace and Fourier transforms over infinite domains power signal processing and control engineering.

Story & open questions

Torricelli's "Gabriel's Horn" (1640s) has finite volume but infinite surface area — fillable with paint, yet impossible to paint. A vivid warning that infinity defies intuition.

By the end you can
  • Evaluate type I (infinite interval) and type II (singular) integrals.
  • Apply the $p$-test.
  • Use the comparison test.

Definitions

Definition

$\int_a^\infty f=\lim_{t\to\infty}\int_a^t f$; if $f$ blows up at $b$, $\int_a^b f=\lim_{t\to b^-}\int_a^t f$. Existence of the limit is convergence.

$p$-test

$\int_1^\infty x^{-p}\,dx$ converges iff $p>1$; $\int_0^1 x^{-p}\,dx$ converges iff $p<1$.

Comparison

Theorem

If $0\le f\le g$ and $\int g$ converges, so does $\int f$; if $\int f$ diverges, so does $\int g$.

Example

$\int_1^\infty\dfrac{dx}{x^2+1}$ converges, by comparison with $x^{-2}$.

Common pitfalls

• Always rewrite as a limit; never substitute $\infty$ directly.
• Watch for a hidden singularity inside the interval.
• Comparison needs nonnegative functions.

Practice

Practice 1

$\int_0^\infty e^{-x}\,dx$.

Answer
$1$.
Practice 2

Does $\int_1^\infty\dfrac{dx}{x}$ converge?

Answer
No; $\ln t\to\infty$ ($p=1$).
Practice 3

Converge or diverge: $\int_1^\infty\dfrac{2+\sin x}{x^2}\,dx$?

Hint
$\le 3/x^2$; converges.
