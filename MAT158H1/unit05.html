The Three Hard Theorems

Intermediate Value, Boundedness, and Extreme Value — the theorems that make calculus work. Each looks obvious, and each genuinely needs the completeness of $\mathbb{R}$ from Unit 2.

Why it matters & where it comes from
Why it exists

"A continuous function that changes sign has a root" and "a continuous function on a closed interval attains a maximum" feel obvious — but they are false without completeness, and proving them is the whole point of building $\mathbb{R}$ carefully.

In the real world

The IVT guarantees that root-finding by bisection converges; the EVT guarantees that optimization problems actually have a solution to find. Both are silent assumptions in countless algorithms.

Story & open questions

Bolzano proved the IVT rigorously in 1817, decades ahead of his time and largely unread. The companion Bolzano–Weierstrass theorem (every bounded sequence has a convergent subsequence) carries both their names.

By the end you can
  • State and prove the Intermediate Value Theorem.
  • State the Boundedness and Extreme Value theorems.
  • Use them to guarantee roots and extrema.

Intermediate Value Theorem

Theorem IVT

If $f$ is continuous on $[a,b]$ and $y$ lies between $f(a)$ and $f(b)$, then $f(c)=y$ for some $c\in[a,b]$.

Proof idea
Suppose $f(a)<0<f(b)$. Let $c=\sup\{x:f(x)<0\}$, which exists by completeness.
Continuity rules out $f(c)<0$ and $f(c)>0$, so $f(c)=0$. ∎

Boundedness and Extreme Value

Theorem EVT

A continuous function on a closed bounded interval $[a,b]$ is bounded and attains a maximum and a minimum value.

Both hypotheses are essential: $1/x$ on $(0,1]$ is continuous but unbounded; $x$ on $(0,1)$ attains neither max nor min.

Common pitfalls

• The interval must be closed and bounded for the EVT.
• The IVT gives existence of $c$, not its value or uniqueness.
• Continuity is required throughout — one jump can break everything.

Practice

Practice 1

Show $x^3-x-1=0$ has a real root.

Answer
$f(1)=-1<0$, $f(2)=5>0$; by IVT a root lies in $(1,2)$.
Practice 2

Show any continuous $f:[0,1]\to[0,1]$ has a fixed point.

Hint
Apply the IVT to $g(x)=f(x)-x$: $g(0)\ge0$, $g(1)\le0$.
Practice 3

Why does the EVT fail for $f(x)=x$ on $(0,1)$?

Answer
The interval is open; the sup $1$ and inf $0$ are never attained.
