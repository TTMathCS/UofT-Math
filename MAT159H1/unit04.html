Taylor's Theorem

Approximating a function by a polynomial, with a precise formula for the error. This is the rigorous heart of "good enough" approximation.

Why it matters & where it comes from
Why it exists

Polynomials are easy; most functions are not. Taylor's theorem says how well a polynomial approximates a function, and exactly how big the error can be.

In the real world

It is how calculators evaluate functions, how physics linearizes hard problems (small-angle $\sin\theta\approx\theta$), and how optimization methods like Newton's are derived.

Story & open questions

Brook Taylor published the series in 1715; Madhava in 14th-century India had found the key cases three centuries earlier. A function can equal its Taylor series only when the remainder tends to zero.

By the end you can
  • Write the Taylor polynomial of a function.
  • State Taylor's theorem with the Lagrange remainder.
  • Bound approximation errors.

The theorem

Theorem Taylor with remainder

$f(x)=\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{f^{(k)}(a)}{k!}(x-a)^k+R_n(x)$, where $R_n(x)=\dfrac{f^{(n+1)}(c)}{(n+1)!}(x-a)^{n+1}$ for some $c$ between $a$ and $x$.

Example

For $e^x$ about $0$: $e^x=1+x+\tfrac{x^2}{2}+\cdots+\tfrac{x^n}{n!}+R_n$, with $R_n=\tfrac{e^c}{(n+1)!}x^{n+1}$.

Error bounds

Example

View $1-\tfrac{x^2}{2}$ as the degree-3 Taylor polynomial of cosine (the cubic coefficient is zero). Then the Lagrange error at $0.1$ is at most $\dfrac{(0.1)^4}{4!}\approx4.2\times10^{-6}$.

Common pitfalls

• The remainder point $c$ is unknown; bound $|f^{(n+1)}|$ over the interval.
• A Taylor series can converge yet not to $f$ — only $R_n\to0$ guarantees equality.
• Centre matters: expanding about a nearby $a$ improves accuracy.

Practice

Practice 1

Write the degree-3 Taylor polynomial of $\sin x$ about $0$.

Answer
$x-\tfrac{x^3}{6}$.
Practice 2

Bound the error of approximating $e^{0.5}$ by $1+0.5+\tfrac{0.25}{2}$.

Hint
$R_2\le\tfrac{e^{0.5}(0.5)^3} {6}\approx0.034$.
Practice 3

Use a Taylor polynomial to estimate $\sqrt{1.1}$.

Answer
$\sqrt{1+x}\approx1+ \tfrac{x}{2}-\tfrac{x^2}{8}$ at $x=0.1$ gives $\approx1.0488$.
