The Riemann Integral

Area, built rigorously from upper and lower sums. The definition is the careful part; once it is in place, the Fundamental Theorem makes computation easy.

Why it matters & where it comes from
Why it exists

"Area under a curve" needs a precise meaning for wiggly functions. Riemann's squeeze of lower and upper sums supplies it, and tells us exactly which functions can be integrated.

In the real world

Accumulated quantities — distance, work, expected value — are integrals. Probability is the area under a density curve, which is why this unit is the gateway to statistics.

Story & open questions

Archimedes integrated by "exhaustion" in ~250 BC; Riemann gave the modern definition in 1854; Lebesgue generalized it in 1902 into measure theory — the foundation of modern probability.

By the end you can
  • Define upper and lower sums and the integral.
  • Apply the integrability criterion $U-L<\varepsilon$.
  • Quote that continuous functions are integrable.
  • Use linearity, additivity, and monotonicity.

Sums and integrability

Definition

For a partition $P$, $L(f,P)=\sum m_i\Delta x_i$ and $U(f,P)=\sum M_i\Delta x_i$ with $m_i,M_i$ the inf/sup on each piece. $f$ is integrable if $\sup_P L=\inf_P U$, the common value being $\int_a^b f$.

Criterion

$f$ is integrable iff for every $\varepsilon>0$ there is $P$ with $U(f,P)-L(f,P)<\varepsilon$.

What is integrable, and properties

Theorem

Continuous (and monotone) functions on $[a,b]$ are integrable. The integral is linear, additive over intervals, monotone, and $\left|\int f\right|\le \int|f|$.

Example

$\int_0^1 x\,dx=\tfrac12$: with $n$ equal pieces, $L=\tfrac{n-1}{2n}$ and $U=\tfrac{n+1}{2n}$ both tend to $\tfrac12$.

Common pitfalls

• $m_i,M_i$ are inf/sup over each subinterval, not endpoint values.
• Integrability is the gap $U-L$ closing — not the existence of an antiderivative.
• Not every bounded function is integrable (e.g. the Dirichlet function).

Practice

Practice 1

Using equal pieces, show $\int_0^1 x^2\,dx=\tfrac13$.

Hint
$U=\tfrac{(n+1)(2n+1)}{6n^2} \to\tfrac13$.
Practice 2

Show the Dirichlet function is not Riemann integrable on $[0,1]$.

Answer
Every $m_i=0$, $M_i=1$, so $L=0$, $U=1$ for all partitions; the gap never closes.
Practice 3

If $1\le f\le 4$ on $[0,2]$, bound $\int_0^2 f$.

Answer
By monotonicity, $2\le\int_0^2 f\le 8$.
