Change of Basis & Similarity

The same linear map looks like different matrices in different bases. Similarity is the relationship between those matrices — and the key to finding a basis where the map is simple.

Why it matters & where it comes from
Why it exists

A clumsy matrix can become diagonal in the right basis. Change of basis is how we hunt for that better viewpoint, and similarity tells us which matrices represent the same map.

In the real world

Picking a better basis is the whole idea behind PCA, Fourier methods, and decoupling systems of differential equations in engineering and finance.

Story & open questions

The search for the "best" basis for a given map leads straight to eigenvalues and the diagonalization problem of Unit 9 — and, when diagonalization fails, to the Jordan form studied in MAT247.

By the end you can
  • Write a change-of-coordinates matrix.
  • Convert a map's matrix between bases.
  • Define similar matrices and list their shared invariants.

Change of coordinates

Definition

If $P$ has the new basis vectors as columns (in old coordinates), then old coordinates $=P\cdot$(new coordinates). For an operator $T:V\to V$ using the same basis change in domain and codomain, $[T]_{\text{new}}=P^{-1}[T]_{\text{old}}P$. More generally, if $P$ changes domain coordinates and $Q$ changes codomain coordinates, then $[T]_{\text{new}}=Q^{-1}[T]_{\text{old}}P$.

Similarity

Definition

$A$ and $B$ are similar if $B=P^{-1}AP$ for some invertible $P$. Similar matrices represent the same linear map in different bases.

Invariants

Similar matrices share determinant, trace, rank, characteristic polynomial, and eigenvalues.

Common pitfalls

• Mind the direction: $[T]_{\text{new}}=P^{-1}[T]_{\text{old}}P$, not the reverse.
• Similar is stronger than "same determinant"; it requires the conjugating $P$.
• Not every matrix is similar to a diagonal one (Unit 9).

Practice

Practice 1

Are similar matrices guaranteed to have the same eigenvalues?

Answer
Yes — they share the characteristic polynomial.
Practice 2

If $B=P^{-1}AP$, relate $\det A$ and $\det B$.

Answer
Equal: $\det B=\det(P)^{-1}\det A\det P=\det A$.
Practice 3

Is the identity similar to any matrix besides itself?

Answer
No — $P^{-1}IP=I$ for all $P$.
