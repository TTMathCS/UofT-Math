Unit 9 · Literature for our Time

Building an Argument

Everything so far produces observations. An essay needs them organised under a claim a reasonable person could dispute, supported in an order that builds, and honest about what could be said against it.

By the end you can
  • Write a thesis that is arguable and specific.
  • Order paragraphs so the argument develops rather than lists.
  • Handle a counter-argument without weakening your case.
  • Tell when a paragraph is not earning its place.
Why it matters & where it comes from
Why it exists

Marks in this course go to argument, not to coverage. A short essay defending one contestable claim well beats a long one that mentions everything.

In the real world

Grant applications, legal submissions, policy memos and design proposals all have this shape: a claim, ordered support, and a serious answer to the strongest objection.

Story & open questions

The five-paragraph essay taught in schools exists to make marking consistent. University essays abandon it because a real argument rarely divides into three equal parts, and the form starts obstructing the thinking it was meant to organise.

The thesis

Rule Three tests
Arguable. Someone informed could disagree. If not, it is an observation.
Specific. About this text, these passages. Not about literature in general.
Answerable in the space. A claim needing a book is not a claim for a thousand words.
Example Sharpening one
Too broad: "The novel explores identity." Nobody disagrees; nothing follows.
Better: "The novel treats identity as performance." Arguable, but still large.
Usable: "The novel's shifts into free indirect discourse occur only when the narrator is describing others, so its account of self-knowledge depends on a distance it never turns on itself."

The third names the evidence in the claim itself, which means the essay already knows what its paragraphs are about.

A thesis is not a topic

"This essay examines the role of memory" announces a subject. "The novel's memories are always someone else's, which makes its nostalgia a borrowed feeling" makes a claim. The difference is whether the sentence could be contradicted.

Structure

Rule Order by argument, not by text

The commonest structural failure is walking through the text in order, commenting as you go. That produces a list. Instead, order the paragraphs by what the argument needs first.

What must the reader accept before the next point makes sense?
Which piece of evidence is strongest, and does it work better early as a foundation or late as confirmation?
Where does the counter-argument fit — usually after the case is established, so answering it strengthens rather than interrupts.
Rule Each paragraph does one job
Opens with a claim that advances the thesis.
Supplies evidence — quotation, close reading.
Analyses how the evidence supports the claim.
Ends by connecting to the argument's next step.

If you cannot say in one sentence what a paragraph contributes, it does not contribute.

Example The deletion test
Delete any paragraph. Is the thesis less supported? If not, it was decoration.
Swap two paragraphs. Does the essay get worse? If not, they are not in an order — they are a list, and the argument is not building.

Both tests take a minute and are the most useful revision moves available.

Counter-argument

Rule Answer the strongest version

Raise the best objection, not a weak one you can dismiss. Setting up an easy target is visible and costs credibility.

State the objection fairly, in its own terms.
Concede whatever is genuinely right about it.
Show why your reading still holds — usually because it accounts for something the objection cannot.
Example A counter-argument doing work

Thesis: a novel's fragmented structure enacts its narrator's unreliability.

Objection: the fragmentation may simply be a period convention, carrying no meaning specific to this narrator.
Concession: true that the technique was widespread, so its presence alone proves nothing.
Answer: but the fragmentation here intensifies precisely in the chapters the narrator was not present for, which a conventional explanation does not predict.

The essay is stronger for having raised it. The objection forced a sharper piece of evidence than the original argument used.

Do not concede your thesis

"Of course, other readings are equally valid" gives away the argument. All readings are not equally valid — some account for more of the text. Acknowledge alternatives, then say why yours is better.

Evidence

Rule How much to quote
Quote the shortest passage that shows what you claim.
If a quotation runs more than two lines, ask which phrase is actually doing the work and cut to it.
Never let a quotation end a paragraph. The analysis is the point, so it goes last.
Example Weighting evidence
One striking example is weaker than three ordinary ones if the claim is about a pattern.
Conversely, a claim about a single crucial moment needs that moment read closely, not three thin gestures.
Match the evidence to the shape of the claim. A pattern claim needs frequency; a turning-point claim needs depth.
Common pitfalls

• A thesis nobody would dispute.
• Walking through the text in order instead of building an argument.
• Paragraphs that summarise rather than advance.
• A straw-man counter-argument.
• Long quotations doing the work the analysis should do.

Practice

Work each fully before opening the answer. These test the moves rather than any particular text.

Practice 1 · Arguable?

Is "the play criticises war" a thesis?

Answer
Barely. Almost no play about war endorses it, so nobody would disagree. Sharpen it: how does it criticise, and what does its method achieve that a direct statement would not?
Practice 2 · Sharpen

Improve: "The poem uses nature imagery to express emotion."

Answer
Something like: "The poem's nature imagery grows more precise as the speaker's account of himself grows vaguer, so the landscape becomes the only thing he can describe honestly." Now there is a claim with a shape.
Practice 3 · Order

Your three paragraphs can be shuffled with no loss. What does that mean?

Answer
They are a list, not an argument. Each should depend on the one before, so reordering breaks something.
Practice 4 · The deletion test

What do you do with a paragraph whose removal changes nothing?

Answer
Remove it, or find the claim it should have been making. Length is not a mark.
Practice 5 · Counter-argument

Why raise an objection you then answer?

Solution
Because an argument that has survived its strongest objection is worth more than one that has not met any. It also demonstrates you know the field of possible readings — and it usually forces you to find sharper evidence.
Practice 6 · Straw man

What is wrong with answering only a weak objection?

Answer
The reader thinks of the strong one you avoided, and now doubts the whole essay. Answering a weak objection is worse than answering none.
Practice 7 · Evidence type

Your claim is about a recurring pattern. What evidence suits it?

Answer
Several instances, briefly handled, to establish frequency — then one read closely to show the mechanism. Frequency plus depth.
Practice 8 · Quotation length

You have quoted five lines. What next?

Answer
Find the phrase actually supporting the claim and cut to it. Long quotations usually signal that the analysis has not decided what it is about.

Course coverage. This unit is part of the complete ENG140Y1 topic map. It should cover the following before you move on:

  • Thesis. Arguable, specific, answerable in the space.
  • Structure. Ordering by argument, and the one-job paragraph.

Textbook/source alignment

Essays are the main assessment in this course. Check Quercus for the topics, length limits and marking rubric — rubrics usually state exactly how argument is weighted against coverage.

Core idea map

Close readings produce observations; a thesis makes a claim they support; structure arranges them so the claim builds; the counter-argument tests it. Unit 10 covers turning that into a finished essay.

Study routine. Write the thesis as a single sentence before drafting, and reread it after every paragraph. If the paragraph does not serve that sentence, one of the two needs changing.

Deep practice extensions

Use these after the main practice set. They test whether the distinctions have actually landed.

Extension 1 · The test

What makes a claim a thesis?

Guide
Someone informed could disagree with it. If not, it is a description.
Extension 2 · Order

How should paragraphs be sequenced?

Guide
By what the argument needs established first — never by where the passages fall in the text.
Extension 3 · Objections

Which objection do you answer?

Guide
The strongest one. A weak objection answered damages the essay more than silence.
Extension 4 · Quotation

How long should a quotation be?

Guide
As short as possible while still showing the claim. The analysis should be longer than the quotation.
