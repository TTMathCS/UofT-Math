Power Series

Functions as infinite polynomials, now on rigorous footing: radius of convergence, and — thanks to uniform convergence — term-by-term calculus inside that radius.

Why it matters & where it comes from
Why it exists

A power series turns a complicated function into something computable, differentiable, and integrable term by term — with the radius of convergence telling you where that is valid.

In the real world

It is how computers evaluate functions and how applied mathematics and machine learning use truncated expansions to approximate and analyze.

Story & open questions

Madhava found power series for sine, cosine, and arctangent around 1400, well before Newton. From these same ideas comes Euler's identity $e^{i\pi}+1=0$, often called the most beautiful equation in mathematics.

By the end you can
  • Find the radius and interval of convergence.
  • Differentiate and integrate power series term by term.
  • Use the common Maclaurin series.

Radius of convergence

Definition

$\sum c_n(x-a)^n$ converges on an interval of radius $R$ about $a$, usually found by the ratio test. Inside $R$ convergence is uniform on closed subintervals, which licenses term-by-term calculus.

Common series

$e^x=\sum\tfrac{x^n}{n!}$, $\dfrac{1}{1-x}=\sum x^n$ ($|x|<1$), $\ln(1+x)=\sum\tfrac{(-1)^{n+1}x^n}{n}$.

Term-by-term calculus

Theorem

Within its radius, a power series may be differentiated and integrated term by term, with the same radius.

Example

Integrating $\dfrac{1}{1+x}=\sum(-x)^n$ gives $\ln(1+x)$ for $|x|<1$.

Common pitfalls

• Endpoints need separate testing; the radius alone does not decide them.
• Term-by-term operations are valid inside the radius, justified by uniform convergence.
• A function need not equal its Taylor series even where the series converges.

Practice

Practice 1

Radius of convergence of $\sum\dfrac{x^n}{n!}$?

Answer
$R=\infty$.
Practice 2

Maclaurin series of $e^{-x^2}$?

Answer
$\sum\tfrac{(-1)^n x^{2n}} {n!}$.
Practice 3

Interval of convergence of $\sum\dfrac{(x-2)^n}{n\,3^n}$?

Answer
$[-1,5)$.
