Determinants

One number that detects invertibility, measures how a map scales volume, and behaves multiplicatively. Simple to state, surprisingly deep to justify.

Why it matters & where it comes from
Why it exists

The determinant answers "is this matrix invertible?" with a single scalar, and geometrically it is the factor by which the map scales area or volume — zero meaning the space collapses.

In the real world

Jacobian determinants rescale integrals under change of variables (essential in probability and physics); determinants signal singular configurations in robotics and graphics.

Story & open questions

Determinants predate matrices — Leibniz and the Japanese mathematician Seki studied them in the 1600s. Computing them efficiently for large matrices is done by the row reduction of Unit 7, not the cofactor formula.

By the end you can
  • Compute determinants by cofactor expansion and row reduction.
  • Use the key properties, including $\det(AB)=\det A\det B$.
  • Connect $\det A\ne0$ with invertibility.

Definition and properties

Definition

The determinant is the unique function of the rows that is linear in each row, alternating (a repeated row gives $0$), and equals $1$ on the identity. Cofactor expansion computes it.

Key properties

$\det(AB)=\det A\det B$; $\det A^{-1}=1/\det A$; $A$ is invertible iff $\det A\ne0$; row swaps flip the sign.

Computing

Example

$\det\!\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)=ad-bc$. For larger matrices, reduce to triangular form and multiply the diagonal (tracking sign changes).

Common pitfalls

• $\det(A+B)\ne\det A+\det B$ in general.
• A row swap multiplies the determinant by $-1$; scaling a row scales it.
• Cofactor expansion is fine by hand but far too slow for large matrices — use row reduction.

Practice

Practice 1

Compute $\det\!\left(\begin{smallmatrix}2&1\\4&3\end{smallmatrix}\right)$.

Answer
$2\cdot3-1\cdot4=2$.
Practice 2

If $\det A=5$ for a $3\times3$ matrix, find $\det(2A)$.

Answer
$2^3\cdot5=40$.
Practice 3

What does $\det A=0$ tell you about $A\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{0}$?

Answer
It has a nonzero solution; $A$ is not invertible.
