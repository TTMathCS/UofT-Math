Infinite Series

Adding infinitely many numbers — when it makes sense and how to test it. The convergence tests here are the working vocabulary for the power series that close the course.

Why it matters & where it comes from
Why it exists

How can an infinite sum be finite, and when? Series make this precise and let functions be represented as sums.

In the real world

Calculators sum series to find $\sin$, $e^x$, and $\pi$; Fourier series compress audio and images (MP3, JPEG); present value in finance is an infinite sum.

Story & open questions

Euler stunned everyone in 1734 with $1+\tfrac14+\tfrac19+\cdots= \tfrac{\pi^2}{6}$. The deepest open problem in mathematics — the Riemann Hypothesis, a one-million-dollar prize — is a statement about a series tied to the primes.

By the end you can
  • Define convergence by partial sums; sum a geometric series.
  • Apply the divergence, comparison, integral, ratio, and root tests.
  • Use the alternating series test and tell absolute from conditional.

Convergence and the basic tests

Definition

$\sum a_n$ converges if the partial sums $s_N=\sum_{n\le N}a_n$ converge. Geometric: $\sum_{n\ge0}r^n=\tfrac{1}{1-r}$ for $|r|<1$.

Tests

Divergence ($a_n\not\to0\Rightarrow$ diverges), comparison, integral, ratio ($L=\lim|a_{n+1}/a_n|<1$), and root.

Alternating and absolute convergence

Theorem

If $b_n\downarrow0$, then $\sum(-1)^n b_n$ converges. If $\sum|a_n|$ converges the series converges absolutely; converging without that is conditional.

Example

$\sum\tfrac{(-1)^n}{n}$ converges conditionally; $\sum\tfrac1n$ diverges.

Common pitfalls

• $a_n\to0$ does not imply convergence (harmonic series).
• Ratio test is inconclusive at $L=1$ — switch tests.
• Check both conditions of the alternating test.

Practice

Practice 1

Does $\sum\dfrac{1}{n^2+1}$ converge?

Answer
Yes, comparison with $1/n^2$.
Practice 2

Test $\sum\dfrac{n}{2^n}$.

Answer
Ratio $\to\tfrac12<1$; converges.
Practice 3

Absolute or conditional: $\sum\dfrac{(-1)^n}{\sqrt n}$?

Answer
Conditional.
