The Mean Value Theorem

The theorem that turns local information about $f'$ into global information about $f$. The proof chain — EVT → Fermat → Rolle → MVT — is one of the most examinable sequences in the course, and every link is short.

By the end you can
  • Prove Fermat's theorem, Rolle's theorem, and the MVT in order.
  • Deduce constancy and monotonicity from the sign of $f'$.
  • Prove inequalities with the MVT.
  • State the Cauchy MVT and Darboux's theorem.
Why it matters & where it comes from
Why it exists

It is the rigorous bridge from a derivative at one point to behaviour across a whole interval — why $f'=0$ everywhere forces $f$ constant, a fact that has no proof without it.

In the real world

Average-speed cameras use it literally; numerical analysis uses it for error bounds; differential equations use it for uniqueness of solutions.

Story & open questions

Named partly for Rolle (1691), himself an early critic of calculus; Cauchy gave the modern statement and the generalized (Cauchy) Mean Value Theorem behind L'Hôpital's rule.

Step 1 — Fermat's theorem

Theorem Fermat

If $f$ has a local maximum or minimum at an interior point $c$ and $f'(c)$ exists, then $f'(c)=0$.

Proof (local max case)
For small $h>0$: $f(c+h)-f(c)\le0$, so the quotient $\dfrac{f(c+h)-f(c)}{h}\le0$, and letting $h\to0^+$ gives $f'(c)\le0$.
For small $h<0$ the quotient is $\ge0$, giving $f'(c)\ge0$. Both hold, so $f'(c)=0$. ∎

Points where $f'=0$ or $f'$ does not exist are the critical points — the only interior candidates for extrema.

Step 2 — Rolle, Step 3 — MVT

Theorem Rolle

If $f$ is continuous on $[a,b]$, differentiable on $(a,b)$, and $f(a)=f(b)$, then $f'(c)=0$ for some $c\in(a,b)$.

Proof
By the EVT (Unit 5), $f$ attains a max and a min on $[a,b]$.
If both happen at endpoints, then max $=$ min (as $f(a)=f(b)$), so $f$ is constant and $f'\equiv0$. Otherwise some extremum is interior, and Fermat gives $f'(c)=0$ there. ∎
Theorem MVT

If $f$ is continuous on $[a,b]$ and differentiable on $(a,b)$, then $$f'(c)=\frac{f(b)-f(a)}{b-a}$$ for some $c\in(a,b)$.

Proof
Tilt the picture flat: let $g(x)=f(x)-\left[f(a)+\dfrac{f(b)-f(a)}{b-a}(x-a)\right]$, the gap between $f$ and its secant line.
$g$ inherits continuity and differentiability, and $g(a)=g(b)=0$; Rolle gives $g'(c)=0$, which rearranges to the MVT. ∎

Consequences

Corollaries on an interval

If $f'=0$ everywhere, $f$ is constant. If $f'>0$ everywhere, $f$ is strictly increasing (similarly $f'<0$, decreasing). If $f'=g'$ everywhere, then $f-g$ is constant.

Proof (constancy)
For any $x_1<x_2$ in the interval, the MVT on $[x_1,x_2]$ gives $f(x_2)-f(x_1)=f'(c)(x_2-x_1)=0$. All values agree. ∎
Example Proving an inequality

$\ln(1+x)<x$ for $x>0$ (using $(\ln t)'=1/t$ from Unit 11):

MVT for $\ln$ on $[1,1+x]$: $\ln(1+x)-\ln1=\tfrac1c\cdot x$ for some $c\in(1,1+x)$.
Since $c>1$, $\tfrac1c<1$, so $\ln(1+x)=\tfrac xc<x$. ∎

Two upgrades worth knowing

Theorem Cauchy MVT

If $f,g$ are continuous on $[a,b]$ and differentiable on $(a,b)$, there is $c\in(a,b)$ with $\big(f(b)-f(a)\big)g'(c)=\big(g(b)-g(a)\big)f'(c)$. (Apply Rolle to $h(x)=f(x)\big(g(b)-g(a)\big)-g(x)\big(f(b)-f(a)\big)$.) This is the engine inside L'Hôpital's rule.

Theorem Darboux

Derivatives have the intermediate value property even when they are not continuous: if $f$ is differentiable on $[a,b]$ and $y$ lies between $f'(a)$ and $f'(b)$, then $f'(c)=y$ for some $c$. So the discontinuous derivative of Unit 6's showpiece still never "jumps."

Common pitfalls

• Check both hypotheses: continuity on the closed interval, differentiability on the open one.
• The MVT gives existence of $c$ — not its value, and not uniqueness.
• "$f'(x_0)>0$ at one point" does not make $f$ increasing near $x_0$; the clean statements are about $f'>0$ on an interval.
• The corollaries need an interval: $f'=0$ on $(0,1)\cup(2,3)$ allows different constants on each piece.

Practice

Original problems written for this guide in the style of Spivak's exercises. Write a full solution before opening the answer.

Practice 1 · Constancy

Prove: $f'=0$ on an interval $\Rightarrow$ $f$ constant there.

Solution
For any $x_1<x_2$, the MVT gives $f(x_2)-f(x_1)=f'(c)(x_2-x_1)=0$, so all values are equal.
Practice 2 · Lipschitz bound

Show $|\sin a-\sin b|\le|a-b|$ for all real $a,b$.

Solution
MVT on $\sin$: $\sin a-\sin b=\cos(c)(a-b)$; take absolute values and use $|\cos c|\le1$.
Practice 3 · Inequality

Prove $e^x\ge1+x$ for all $x$ (using $(e^x)'=e^x$ from Unit 11).

Solution
$h(x)=e^x-1-x$ has $h(0)=0$ and $h'(x)=e^x-1$, which is negative for $x<0$ and positive for $x>0$. So $h$ decreases then increases: $x=0$ is a global minimum and $h\ge0$ everywhere.
Practice 4 · Rolle by hand

Verify Rolle's theorem for $f(x)=x^2-4x+3$ on $[1,3]$ and find every guaranteed $c$.

Answer
$f(1)=f(3)=0$, $f$ is a polynomial, and $f'(x)=2x-4$ vanishes exactly at $c=2\in(1,3)$.
Practice 5 · Exactly one root

Show $x^3+3x+1=0$ has exactly one real root.

Solution
$f(-1)=-3<0<1=f(0)$ gives a root (IVT). If there were two, Rolle would give a point with $f'=0$; but $f'(x)=3x^2+3\ge3>0$. So exactly one.
Practice 6 · Bounded slope

Suppose $|f'(x)|\le M$ for all $x$ in an interval. Prove $|f(x)-f(y)|\le M|x-y|$ for all $x,y$ there.

Solution
For $x\ne y$ the MVT gives $f(x)-f(y)=f'(c)(x-y)$; take absolute values. (This is called a Lipschitz bound.)
Practice 7 · Trig inequality

Prove $\tan x>x$ for $x\in\left(0,\tfrac\pi2\right)$.

Solution
$g(x)=\tan x-x$ has $g(0)=0$ and $g'(x)=\sec^2x-1=\tan^2x>0$ on $\left(0,\tfrac\pi2\right)$, so $g$ is strictly increasing from $0$.
Practice 8 · Counting zeros

Prove a polynomial of degree $n$ has at most $n$ real zeros.

Solution
$n+1$ distinct zeros would give, by Rolle between consecutive ones, $n$ zeros of $p'$, then $n-1$ of $p''$, …, finally a zero of the $n$th derivative — which is the nonzero constant $n!\,a_n$. Contradiction.

Deep practice extensions

Use these after the main practice set. They are designed to force definition-level reasoning, theorem selection, and mixed application rather than routine calculation only.

Extension 1 · MVT statement

State the hypotheses and conclusion of the Mean Value Theorem.

Guide
Continuity on $[a,b]$ and differentiability on $(a,b)$ imply some $c\in(a,b)$ satisfies $f\prime(c)=(f(b)-f(a))/(b-a)$.
Extension 2 · Constant derivative

Prove if $f\prime=0$ on an interval then $f$ is constant.

Guide
For any two points, MVT gives the difference quotient equals $0$, so the function values are equal.
Extension 3 · Bound

If $|f\prime(x)|\le3$ on $[a,b]$, prove $|f(b)-f(a)|\le3|b-a|$.

Guide
Apply MVT and take absolute values.
Extension 4 · Hypothesis failure

Why does Rolle fail for $f(x)=|x|$ on $[-1,1]$?

Guide
The endpoint values match, but $f$ is not differentiable at $0$, and there is no point with derivative $0$ in the differentiable parts.
