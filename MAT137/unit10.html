Applications of the Integral

Once the integral is "add up infinitely many tiny pieces," geometric and physical quantities follow: area, volume, average value, and arc length. The skill is setting up the right integral.

By the end you can
  • Find the area between two curves.
  • Compute volumes of revolution by disks/washers and shells.
  • Find the average value of a function.
  • Set up an arc-length integral.
Why it matters & where it comes from
Why it exists

Once you can integrate, geometry and physics open up — areas, volumes, lengths, and averages all become single integrals.

In the real world

Volumes of revolution show up in manufacturing and 3D printing; CT and MRI scanners reconstruct the body by integrating along rays; arc length is path planning and GPS. A signal's average value gives its DC component, while its RMS value is the square root of the average of its square.

Story & open questions

Archimedes found the volume of a sphere this way and was so proud he asked for a sphere-in-a-cylinder on his tombstone. In 1615 Kepler worked out the volumes of wine barrels — an early, very practical burst of integration.

Area between curves

Formula

If $f\ge g$ on $[a,b]$, the area between them is $\displaystyle\int_a^b\big(f(x)-g(x)\big)\,dx.$

Example

Between $y=x^2$ and $y=2-x^2$: they meet at $x=\pm1$, and $\int_{-1}^{1}(2-2x^2)\,dx=\big[2x-\tfrac{2}{3}x^3\big]_{-1}^{1}=\tfrac83.$

Volumes of revolution

Two methods

Disks/washers (slices $\perp$ to the axis): $V=\displaystyle\int_a^b\pi\big(R^2-r^2\big)\,dx.$   Shells (slices $\parallel$): for a region in $x\ge0$ rotated about the $y$-axis, $V=\displaystyle\int_a^b 2\pi x\,h(x)\,dx$. In general, use each slice's distance from the axis as its radius.

Example

Revolve $y=\sqrt{x}$, $0\le x\le4$, about the $x$-axis: $V=\int_0^4\pi(\sqrt{x})^2\,dx=\pi\int_0^4 x\,dx=8\pi.$

Average value

Definition

The average value of $f$ on $[a,b]$ is $\bar f=\dfrac{1}{b-a}\displaystyle\int_a^b f.$ The Mean Value Theorem for integrals says a continuous $f$ attains this value somewhere.

Arc length

Formula

If $f'$ is continuous on $[a,b]$, the length of $y=f(x)$ over $[a,b]$ is $L=\displaystyle\int_a^b\sqrt{1+\big(f'(x)\big)^2}\,dx.$

Common pitfalls

• Find intersection points before integrating; check which curve is on top.
• For washers, subtract the inner radius squared, not the radii then squared.
• Match the slicing method to the axis to avoid impossible integrals.

Practice

Problems adapted from the MAT137 question bank by Alfonso Gracia-Saz, used under CC BY-SA 4.0. Write full solutions before opening the answer.

Practice 1 · Area

Find the area between $y=x$ and $y=x^2$.

Answer
They meet at $0$ and $1$; $\int_0^1(x-x^2)\,dx=\tfrac12-\tfrac13=\tfrac16.$
Practice 2 · Shells

Revolve $y=x^2$, $0\le x\le1$, about the $y$-axis using shells.

Answer
$V=\int_0^1 2\pi x\cdot x^2\,dx=2\pi\cdot\tfrac14=\tfrac{\pi}{2}.$
Practice 3 · Average value

Average value of $f(x)=\sin x$ on $[0,\pi]$.

Answer
$\dfrac{1}{\pi}\int_0^\pi \sin x\,dx=\dfrac{2}{\pi}.$
Practice 4 · Disk formula

Let $f$ be positive on $[a,b]$. Write the volume when the region under $f$ is rotated about the $x$-axis.

Answer
$V=\displaystyle\int_a^b \pi\,[f(x)]^2\,dx$ — a stack of disks of radius $f(x)$.
Practice 5 · Sphere

Rotate the circle $x^2+y^2=R^2$ about the $x$-axis and find the sphere's volume by disks.

Solution
$V=\int_{-R}^{R} \pi(R^2-x^2)\,dx=\pi\!\left[R^2x-\tfrac{x^3}{3}\right]_{-R}^{R} =\tfrac43\pi R^3.$
Practice 6 · Pyramid

Find the volume of a pyramid of height $H$ with a square base of side $L$, using slices parallel to the base.

Solution
A slice a distance $y$ from the apex has side $Ly/H$, so $V=\int_0^H\!\left(\tfrac{Ly}{H}\right)^2dy =\tfrac{L^2H}{3}.$
Practice 7 · Washer

Rotate the region between $y=\sqrt{x}$ and $y=x$ about the $x$-axis.

Answer
$V=\int_0^1\pi\!\left[x-x^2 \right]dx=\pi\!\left(\tfrac12-\tfrac13\right)=\tfrac{\pi}{6}.$
Practice 8 · Washer

Rotate the first-quadrant region between $y=x^3$ and $y=\sqrt{32x}$ about the $x$-axis.

Solution
They meet at $x=2$ ($x^5=32$). $V=\int_0^2\pi\!\left[32x-x^6\right]dx =\pi\!\left[16x^2-\tfrac{x^7}{7}\right]_0^2=\tfrac{320\pi}{7}.$
