1. Calculus vs Analysis: which stream

In 2025-26 U of T split both first-year theory streams into two half-courses. The old full-year MAT137 became MAT148 + MAT149. The old full-year MAT157 became MAT158 + MAT159. The real choice is between two streams.

Calculus stream (was MAT137Y1)Analysis stream (was MAT157Y1)
Fall halfMAT148H1 — Calculus I with ProofsMAT158H1 — Analysis 1A
Winter halfMAT149H1 — Calculus II with ProofsMAT159H1 — Analysis 1B
Proof companionMAT138H1 optionalMAT138H1 recommended concurrently
AudienceCS, econ, physics, stats, math majorStudents set on a math specialist
Proof levelIntroduces proofs, simpler onesYou build and own complex proofs
Required forMost quantitative majorsMandatory for Mathematics Specialist

Both cover the same skeleton: limits, continuity, derivatives, mean value theorem, integrals, fundamental theorem, transcendental functions, sequences, series, Taylor. The gap is rigor and abstraction, not the topic list.

  • 148/149: conceptual approach, computation plus problem solving, proofs introduced. Strong default for a quantitative field without the brutal load.
  • 158/159: the hard track. Same topics proven more fully, plus harder material (Cauchy sequences, uniform convergence). Descendant of the old MAT157. Worth it only if the goal is mathematics itself.

Rule of thumb: heading to math specialist, take 158/159. Heading to CS/physics/econ/stats and want rigor without the heaviest load, take 148/149. MAT240H1 (Algebra I), proof-based linear algebra, pairs naturally with the analysis stream. 158/159 plus MAT240 is the classic math specialist first year.

2. Path for a stats-specialist, PhD-bound math major

This path suits a math major leaning toward the Statistics Specialist, aiming for a PhD in statistics and probability and then quant finance or AI. It keeps all three exits open.

Verdict on first year

Take the analysis stream, MAT158H1 + MAT159H1, not 148/149. Pair it with MAT240 (Algebra I). Graduate probability and theoretical statistics are measure-theoretic, built on real analysis. The hard stream builds that maturity now, while it is cheap; backfilling analysis later is painful. Linear algebra is the most-used tool in both ML and quant finance, so own it rigorously.

Course spine

Shape, not exact codes. Verify against the current calendar since codes shift.

  • Year 1: MAT158/159 (Analysis), MAT240 + MAT247 (Algebra I/II).
  • Year 2: rigorous multivariable analysis (MAT257Y1), proof-based probability and inference (STA257/261), intro CS and data structures (CSC110/111/148).
  • Year 3: real analysis / intro measure theory (MAT337), probability (STA347), mathematical statistics (STA452/453), machine learning (CSC311), optimization, complex analysis (MAT334).
  • Year 4: a graduate measure-theoretic probability or stochastic processes course, stochastic calculus, STA414/CSC412 (probabilistic ML), a research or reading course.

This mix keeps three exits open: PhD (analysis, measure theory, research), quant finance (stochastic calculus, probability, C++), AI (linear algebra, optimization, ML).

Practices

  1. Write proofs daily. The PhD bottleneck is proof maturity, not computation. Master 158/159, do not just pass it.
  2. Get into research early. Aim for an NSERC USRA by end of year 2. Research and a strong letter matter more than raw GPA for stats PhD admission.
  3. Two programming tracks: Python/R for stats and ML, C++ if quant finance stays on the table. Start Python now.
  4. Protect the GPA in the proof courses: analysis, algebra, probability.
  5. Putnam and problem solving. Optional, but proof speed compounds.
  6. Build a portfolio in this repo: worked analysis sets, probability simulations, a small ML or pricing project.

3. Degree and program requirements

Mandatory degree rules

  • 20.0 credits total to graduate, at least 10.0 from Arts & Science.
  • 13.0 credits at 200-level or higher, of which at least 6.0 at 300/400-level.
  • No more than 15.0 credits in one three-letter subject code.
  • Cumulative GPA at least 1.85 to graduate.
  • Program combination, pick one: one Specialist, or two Majors, or one Major plus two Minors. A Statistics Specialist alone satisfies this; adding a Mathematics Major or Minor is natural given the shared core.

Program enrolment (POSt)

The PhD-track program is the limited-enrolment Specialist in Statistical Science: Theory and Methods. The 2026-27 calendar has two entry routes:

  • With 4.0 to 8.5 completed credits: complete STA130H1, an accepted introductory CSC course, MAT223H1/MAT224H1/MAT240H1, and an accepted first-year calculus stream (minimum 63% in the listed calculus courses). Admission uses a variable minimum average in STA130H1 and calculus.
  • With 9.0 or more completed credits: complete an accepted introductory CSC course, MAT223H1/MAT224H1/MAT240H1, MAT237Y1/MAT257Y1, and STA257H1 plus STA261H1 (minimum 63% in each STA course). Admission uses a variable minimum average in STA257H1, STA261H1, and MAT237Y1/MAT257Y1.

The variable cutoff changes with spaces and applicants. Verify requirements in the current Statistical Sciences calendar before enrolment.

Breadth requirement

Need 4.0 breadth credits, in one of two patterns:

  • 1.0 credit in each of 4 of the 5 categories, or
  • 1.0 credit in each of 3 categories plus 0.5 in each of the other 2.

Categories: (1) Creative and Cultural Representations, (2) Thought, Belief, and Behaviour, (3) Society and Its Institutions, (4) Living Things and Their Environment, (5) The Physical and Mathematical Universes.

Math, stats, and CS fill category 5 for free. Make the rest do double duty:

  • SII: ECO101 + ECO102. Covers a category and feeds the finance exit.
  • TBB: a logic, philosophy of science, or ethics course. Sharpens proofs; ethics also matters for AI.
  • CCR: one writing-heavy humanities course. Communication is most quants' weak spot; treat it as a real skill.
  • LTE (if using the 4-category pattern): intro psychology or cognitive science pairs well with AI interests.

Finish breadth in years 1 and 2. First-Year Foundations seminars (199 courses) carry breadth and put you in front of a professor early.

4. Living in residence

Most first-year students at the St. George campus live in a college or campus residence. The math, stats, and CS buildings sit at the centre of campus, so nearly every residence is a short walk from class. Placement depends on your college and the residence process.

Common residence options

  • New College — Wilson Hall, Wetmore Hall, 45 Willcocks.
  • University College — Morrison Hall and Sir Daniel Wilson Residence.
  • Victoria College — Burwash Hall, Annesley Hall, Margaret Addison.
  • Trinity College — Trinity and St. Hilda's residences.
  • St. Michael's College, Innis College, and Woodsworth College residences.
  • Chestnut Residence — large and open to students from any college.
  • Graduate House and CampusOne — apartment-style options for upper-year or graduate students.

Use the geography

Most St. George residences are a 5-15 minute walk from Robarts Library, Sidney Smith Hall, the Bahen Centre (CS and Math), Gerstein Science Library, and the athletic facilities. Schedule office hours and study blocks back to back with class so there is no commute between them.

Study spots for proof work

Rooms are for sleep and light review. For deep proof work: (1) your own college library, usually quiet and closest, the daily default; (2) Gerstein, best for long STEM sessions and group rooms; (3) Robarts, open late but intense near exams. Form a proof study group in the first month and book a room weekly.

Dining and food

Most colleges run a dining hall (for example, New College's Audrey Taylor Dining Room). Meal plans are usually declining-balance, so track the balance and do not splurge week one. Chinatown, Kensington, and Koreatown are nearby for cheap variety.

College resources

  • Your college registrar's office: financial aid and OSAP, petitions, advising, in-course scholarships and bursaries. Register early; the college is a support base, not just the department.
  • Writing centre: free one-on-one help for the breadth writing course and any reports.
  • College mentorship and First-Year Foundations seminars.

Community

A big campus is easy to feel anonymous in; residence is the antidote in first year. Get to know your floor don or RA, join residence council events, join one academic club early (Mathematics Union, Statistics Students' Union, a data-science or quant club), and one non-academic thing for balance.

5. Staying safe and healthy downtown

The campus is in the core of downtown Toronto. Generally safe, but the real risks are ordinary: theft, late-night walks alone, student scams, and burnout.

Save these contacts first

  • Emergency, anywhere: 911.
  • Campus Safety and TravelSafer walk escort: 416-978-7233 (SAFE), 24/7.
  • Download the UofT Safety app (virtual walk-home escort, emergency call, alerts).
  • Health and Wellness Centre: 416-978-8030.
  • Good2Talk, free 24/7 student helpline: 1-866-925-5454.
  • MySSP, free 24/7 counselling in many languages: 1-844-451-9700.
  • 9-8-8, suicide and crisis line (call or text), Canada-wide.

Personal safety

  • Use TravelSafer for late walks from Robarts or labs. Free, meant to be used.
  • Stick to lit main streets (St. George, Harbord, College, Spadina); avoid Queen's Park and Philosopher's Walk alone after dark.
  • Stay aware: low earbud volume, off the phone at night, trust instinct.

Property, the most common actual crime

  • Never leave a laptop or bag unattended in a library or cafe, not even briefly. Library laptop theft is the most common thing students get hit by.
  • Use a solid U-lock for bikes, lock frame and wheel, register the bike.
  • Keep the residence room locked even for short trips.

Scams targeting students

  • No real agency (CRA, immigration, police) demands payment by gift cards, crypto, or e-transfer, or threatens arrest by phone. Hang up.
  • Ignore virtual-kidnapping and fake-emergency money calls; verify with family.
  • Watch phishing about tuition, OSAP, or accounts; log in through the official site, never an emailed link.

Health

  • Register with Health and Wellness as the campus family doctor; walk-in clinics and pharmacies are all over downtown for off-hours.
  • Three short workouts a week at the campus athletic facilities are the cheapest stress and sleep medicine there is.
  • Eat real meals, not just coffee during problem sets.
  • Real winter boots and coat by November. Toronto winters are long and grey; get daylight, consider vitamin D, and watch for the winter slump. Weeks of low mood is a Health and Wellness conversation, not a toughen-up problem.

Mental health is a safety issue too

The workload is a marathon and isolation is the real danger. Using support early is smart, not weak. Good2Talk and MySSP are free, 24/7, anonymous; Health and Wellness handles on-campus counselling; for any immediate crisis, 9-8-8 or 911. Pick one trusted person to text on a bad night before you need to.
