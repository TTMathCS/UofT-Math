About this course

Where analysis gets deep.

MAT159H1 is "Analysis 1B": integrals, the fundamental theorem, elementary transcendental functions, integration techniques, Taylor's theorem, series, uniform convergence, and power series. It continues from MAT158H1. This guide uses Spivak's Calculus as a rigorous companion. Uniform convergence is the key new idea and the bridge to later real analysis.

Textbook Spine

MAT159H1 continues the Spivak-style analysis habit: every computation is attached to a convergence or approximation claim. The public calendar confirms the topics; this guide uses Spivak's Calculus as the companion text and treats the current syllabus as the authority for exact assigned readings.

Unit-to-textbook alignment
Integration depth

Units 1-2 pair with the text's integration methods and improper-integral material. The goal is not only to evaluate an integral but to justify when the integral exists and which comparison proves convergence.

Sequences and approximation

Units 3-4 pair with sequence convergence and Taylor's theorem. Treat Cauchy arguments, monotonicity, boundedness, and remainder estimates as the core proof techniques.

Series and functions

Units 5-7 pair with infinite series, uniform convergence, and power series. The main theme is permission: when may limits, sums, derivatives, and integrals be interchanged?

Course-wide challenge bank

Use these as mixed checks after the local unit practice. Every answer should include the test or theorem that justifies the conclusion.

Challenge 1 · Improper integral

For which $p$ does $\displaystyle\int_e^\infty \dfrac{dx}{x(\ln x)^p}$ converge?

Solution sketch
Use $u=\ln x$, $du=dx/x$. The integral becomes $\int_1^\infty u^{-p}\,du$, which converges exactly when $p>1$.
Challenge 2 · Taylor error

Use Taylor's theorem to show that $|\sin x-(x-x^3/6)|\le |x|^5/120$ for all real $x$.

Solution sketch
Taylor's theorem with remainder through degree $3$ gives a fifth-order bound because the fourth-degree coefficient is zero and the fifth derivative is bounded in absolute value by $1$. Hence the remainder is at most $|x|^5/5!$.
Challenge 3 · Uniform convergence

Show $x^n\to0$ uniformly on $[0,a]$ when $0<a<1$, but not uniformly on $[0,1]$.

Solution sketch
On $[0,a]$, $\sup |x^n|=a^n\to0$. On $[0,1]$, the pointwise limit is $0$ for $x<1$ and $1$ at $x=1$, which is discontinuous; a uniform limit of continuous functions is continuous.
Challenge 4 · Power series

Find the radius of convergence of $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{n^2x^n}{3^n}$.

Solution sketch
The ratio of consecutive absolute terms tends to $|x|/3$, so the series converges for $|x|<3$ and diverges for $|x|>3$. The radius is $3$.
