Fields & Vector Spaces

Linear algebra starts not with matrices but with axioms: a field of scalars and a vector space over it. Everything later is a consequence of these rules.

Why it matters & where it comes from
Why it exists

Defining a vector space abstractly — by axioms, not as arrows or columns — means one theorem covers polynomials, functions, and data vectors at once. Abstraction buys reach.

In the real world

Vector spaces over $\mathbb{R}$ and $\mathbb{C}$ are the language of machine learning (data as vectors), quantum mechanics, and signal processing; finite fields power error-correcting codes and cryptography.

Story & open questions

Grassmann and Peano formalized vector spaces in the late 1800s; the abstract axiomatic view took hold in the 20th century and is now the default language of pure and applied mathematics alike.

By the end you can
  • State the field axioms and give examples ($\mathbb{R}$, $\mathbb{C}$, $\mathbb{Z}_p$).
  • State the vector space axioms.
  • Verify whether a given set is a vector space.

Fields

Definition Field

A set with $+$ and $\times$ satisfying the usual associative, commutative, distributive, identity, and inverse laws (every nonzero element invertible). Examples: $\mathbb{Q},\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C}$, and $\mathbb{Z}_p$ for prime $p$.

Vector spaces

Definition Vector space

A set $V$ over a field $F$ with vector addition and scalar multiplication obeying eight axioms (associativity and commutativity of $+$, a zero vector, additive inverses, and the scalar-multiplication and distributive laws).

Examples

$F^n$, the polynomials $P(F)$, the $m\times n$ matrices, and the functions $F\to F$ are all vector spaces.

Common pitfalls

• Closure under both operations must be checked first.
• The zero vector must actually lie in the set.
• Scalars come from a field — over $\mathbb{Z}_p$, arithmetic wraps around.

Practice

Practice 1

Is $\{(x,y):x+y=0\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^2$ a vector space?

Answer
Yes — closed under addition and scalars, contains $0$.
Practice 2

Is $\{(x,y):x+y=1\}$ a vector space?

Answer
No — it does not contain $(0,0)$.
Practice 3

Compute $3\times 4$ in $\mathbb{Z}_5$.

Answer
$12\equiv2\pmod 5$.
