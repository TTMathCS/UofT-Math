Systems & Elementary Operations

Row reduction is the computational workhorse — it reveals rank, solves systems, and inverts matrices, all by the same three elementary moves.

Why it matters & where it comes from
Why it exists

Row reduction is the concrete algorithm behind the abstract theory: it turns "does a solution exist?" into a mechanical procedure that also computes rank and inverses.

In the real world

Solving $A\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{b}$ is everywhere — circuit analysis, least squares regression, computer graphics, and the linear solves at the core of scientific computing.

Story & open questions

The method is called Gaussian elimination, though versions appear in a Chinese text from around 200 BC. How fast matrices can be multiplied and inverted is still an open research frontier.

By the end you can
  • Apply the three elementary row operations.
  • Read rank and solutions from row-echelon form.
  • Invert a matrix by row reduction.

Elementary operations

Definition

Swap two rows, scale a row by a nonzero scalar, or add a multiple of one row to another. Each corresponds to multiplying by an invertible elementary matrix.

Theorem

Row operations preserve the solution set of a system and the row space; the number of nonzero rows in echelon form is the rank.

Solving and inverting

Example Solution types

After reduction: a pivot in every variable column gives a unique solution; a free column gives infinitely many; a row $0=1$ gives none.

To invert $A$: row-reduce $[A\,|\,I]$ to $[I\,|\,A^{-1}]$.

Common pitfalls

• Scaling by zero is not allowed.
• A row $0=1$ (inconsistent) means no solution; do not keep reducing for one.
• Free variables parametrize infinitely many solutions — write the general form.

Practice

Practice 1

How many solutions has a $3\times3$ system whose matrix has rank $2$ and is consistent?

Answer
Infinitely many — one free variable.
Practice 2

When is $A\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{b}$ guaranteed solvable for every $\mathbf{b}$?

Answer
When $A$ has full row rank (rank $=$ number of rows).
Practice 3

What does a row of zeros in echelon form indicate?

Answer
That row contributes no pivot. Rank is the total number of nonzero echelon rows; a zero row alone does not prove that rank dropped by exactly one.
